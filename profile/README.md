# MODID – Attack-proof development of cyber-physical systems using Model-based Defense in Depth

MODID investigates how cyber-physical systems, such as modern production plants or vehicles, can be made resilient against cyberattacks by design.
Following a model-based defense-in-depth approach, we take attacks on subsystems into account already during development, so that a system stays protected even if one layer of defense is breached.

MODID is a DFG Research Unit at [Paderborn University](https://www.uni-paderborn.de) and the [Heinz Nixdorf Institute](https://www.hni.uni-paderborn.de), bringing together computer science, mechanical engineering and economics.
It is led by Prof. Dr. Eric Bodden and Prof. Dr. Iris Gräßler.
This organization hosts the software, research artifacts and paper sources produced in the research unit.
Released results are published as open source.

*Official title (German): „Angriffssichere Entwicklung Cyber-physischer Systeme mittels Model-based Defense in Depth“*

## Research focus
- **Security by design**: defense strategies that anticipate attacks on subsystems during engineering and production, not only in the finished system.
- **Defense in depth**: multiple layers of protection, so that the system remains secure even if one layer is breached, and its resilience can be predicted under different attack conditions.
- **Security metrics** at the level of system architecture and program code to estimate the attack resilience of cyber-physical systems.
- **Joint evaluation**: the wheel and brake system of the [UPBracing](https://upbracing.com) Formula Student car, including its additively manufactured wheel carrier and the software that interacts with it.
